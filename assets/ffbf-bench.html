The wiring never changes

Each of the 300 KCs reads a fixed random slice of the input — round(0.12 · 64) = 8 lines, drawn once from a seeded shuffle. That is the hash. Similar inputs light similar detectors, which is the whole reason a near-duplicate scores low.

Only the weights move

On add(): the k winners get w ← max(w·(1−δ), 1e-3), everyone else w ← min(w+ε, 1). The score is the mean weight under the winners, read before the write.

Novelty is relative

The verdict compares the score to the mean of a rolling window of recent scores, not to a fixed number. A stream that drifts drags its own baseline along.

A faithful JavaScript port of ffbf::FFBF — same seeded sparse projection, same top-k, same update rule, same tick shapes. Sized down to m = 300 so one synapse is still a visible bar. δ 0.5 · ε 0.03 · k 15 · window 24.
